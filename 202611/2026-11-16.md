#### Abstract 原文

> In this work, we study on-policy distillation (OPD) for transferring agentic coding capabilities from a large teacher model to a smaller student model. Prior work has focused primarily on reasoning and short-horizon agentic tasks. We present SWE-OPD, a minimal OPD study for software engineering, where the student interacts with executable repositories and the teacher supervises the prefixes visited by the student. Across three teacher--student pairs, two training sources, and three SWE benchmarks, we find that on-policy supervision transfers coding capability: supervision from Qwen3.6-27B increases the SWE-bench Pro resolve rate of Qwen3.5-9B from 35.9% to 39.4%. Refreshing trajectories is not strictly required either, as an offline variant with cached teacher scores still improves over the student baselines while cutting end-to-end GPU-hours by 59.4%--72.6% and halving peak GPU allocation, at benchmark-dependent accuracy. Furthermore, we extend the offline variant to corss-tokenizer scenarios: distilling GLM-4.5-Air from DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731 raises its SWE-bench Pro and Multilingual resolve rate from 35.8% to 39.7% and from 41.8% to 46.2%, around 4% and 4.6% improvement, respectively. We also find that a decreasing loss, however, does not necessarily indicate improved agent performance: under top-$K$ reverse-KL loss, one student exhibits severe repetition despite a decreasing loss. We identify a support-mass gradient term as a potential mechanism underlying this failure.

#### Abstract 原文

> Flow-based vision-language-action (VLA) models achieve strong performance in robotics through imitation learning, yet fine-tuning them with on-policy reinforcement learning (RL) is limited. In group-based RL methods like Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), every rollout step requires multi-step ODE integration, and the sampler's limited stochasticity leads to rollout groups with low variance. We propose VLA-SSD, which addresses both high inference latency and limited stochasticity. In particular, our method self-distills a pretrained flow-matching action head into a stochastic one-step policy by conditioning a single-jump generator on a Brownian path. We derive a conditional action density via our stochastic one-step policy, enabling efficient post-training via likelihood-ratio RL algorithms. We show that distilling VLA-SSD from the $π_0$ VLA reduces the action head's forward-pass time by a factor of 7.7, at the cost of a 5.5% relative decrease in the success rate on the LIBERO benchmark. However, by leveraging the increased sample diversity of VLA-SSD during group generation, post-training a $π_{0.5}$ VLA using GRPO yields a 20.6% relative improvement.

#### Abstract 原文

> On-policy distillation (OPD) provides dense token-level supervision along student-generated trajectories, yet the learning benefits of longer trajectories may not scale proportionally with their computational cost. We investigate how supervision utility evolves with trajectory length. Controlled experiments reveal diminishing returns from additional supervised tokens, while showing that later positions remain learnable and exhibit substantial cross-position supervision transfer. Gradient analysis further identifies shared update components across response positions and diverse position-specific residuals whose cross-rollout consistency decreases at greater depths. Motivated by these findings, we propose Stochastic Horizons On-Policy Distillation (SH-OPD), which reduces redundant supervision by randomly varying rollout horizons while preserving access to deeper response states. SH-OPD samples a shared horizon for each training batch, enabling efficient parallel generation without per-sequence horizon scheduling overhead. We characterize the resulting reweighting bias and derive a compute-reweighting trade-off under standard optimization assumptions. Experiments on mathematical reasoning and code generation demonstrate that SH-OPD achieves slightly higher mathematical reasoning accuracy at approximately 45% of the per-update cost of standard OPD, while maintaining competitive code generation performance at around 60% of the baseline cost. These results highlight the benefits of efficient supervision allocation across response depths.

#### Abstract 原文

> LLM unlearning aims to erase targeted knowledge from large language models while preserving their utility. However, many existing methods suppress target knowledge without explicitly teaching the model how to respond to queries about it. We propose LEAF-OPD (LEArning to Forget from Privileged Contexts via On-Policy Distillation), in which a frozen teacher provides token-level supervision on student-generated responses. On forget data, the teacher uses privileged contexts (instructions not to reveal knowledge about forget targets) to guide forgetting. On retain data, the teacher provides supervision without privileged context to preserve model utility. The student operates without privileged context in both cases, learning how to respond when encountering knowledge that should be forgotten. A key challenge is that the teacher often provides effective supervision only at the first few tokens of student trajectories. We introduce Supervision Signal (SS) to measure corrective supervision over a specified token interval, evaluating the entire trajectory (SS1) and its second half (SS0.5) to assess overall strength and late-stage persistence. When supervision is weak, lightweight SFT strengthens the teacher's context-conditioned refusal behavior, substantially increasing both scores. We evaluate LEAF-OPD on three widely used benchmarks: TOFU, RWKU, and MUSE-News. On TOFU, LEAF-OPD achieves the best Forget Quality, Model Utility, and Forget ROUGE-L while maintaining competitive retain performance. On RWKU, it outperforms the second-best method by 34% in Forget Quality while maintaining competitive Retain Quality. These results demonstrate the effectiveness of privileged-context supervision with on-policy token-level distillation for LLM unlearning.

#### Abstract 原文

> Zero-shot cross-game generalization aims to consolidate accessible source-game experts into a single vision-language model (VLM) that controls unseen games from raw pixels without target-game parameter updates. While multi-teacher on-policy distillation (MOPD) mitigates offline exposure bias and single-game reinforcement learning (RL) overfitting, scaling it to autoregressive VLMs faces two bottlenecks. First, token-to-action misalignment from subword length bias distorts discrete action probabilities and uncertainty margins. Second, multi-teacher policy conflict arises as source teachers under incompatible dynamics issue contradictory closed-loop corrections, causing negative transfer. We propose CAMOPD (Conflict-Aware Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation), a post-training framework that resolves multi-teacher policy conflict within an aligned semantic action space. CAMOPD first uses complete-action alignment to normalize command completion likelihoods over each game's legal action set, forming a calibrated categorical policy that provides reliable uncertainty margins for selective on-policy retention and visual specialist probes that can be evaluated across source games. To resolve policy conflict without target-game data, conflict-aware multi-teacher consolidation cross-evaluates these specialists across source games to capture both visual identifiability and inter-teacher compatibility, upweighting compatible teachers while suppressing conflicting corrections. On GameWorld's temporal and directional transfer suites with Qwen3.5-4B, CAMOPD achieves 26/30 (86.7%) and 24/30 (80.0%) Task-1 zero-shot success on unseen GeoDash and Doodle Jump, outperforming prompting, weight-merging, and multi-task baselines.
