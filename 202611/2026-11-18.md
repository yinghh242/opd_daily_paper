### 1. WAM-Policy: World Action Models with On-Policy Distillation for Quadrupedal Loco-Manipulation

- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24011
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.24011
- Authors: Junlin Long, Xu Deng, Yiran Wang, Yue Yang, Yang Zhao, Zeyu Zhang
- 作者单位：待全文阅读确认

- arXiv updated: 2026-11-18
- Categories: cs.RO, cs.CV

#### 与 OPD / OPSD 的相关性依据

标题或摘要中明确出现强相关短语：on-policy distillation。

#### Abstract 原文

> Arm-free quadrupeds can perform loco-manipulation, walking on three legs while manipulating with a front leg, yet no existing policy for it follows language instructions. World action models (WAMs) have emerged as effective language-conditioned manipulation policies, but are pretrained for robot arms and sample slowly. WAM-Policy turns one WAM into a quadrupedal loco-manipulation policy via two stages. The first, Cross-Morphology Policy Adaptation and Acceleration, adapts the arm-centric WAM to quadrupeds and distills the transferred policy into a student with one video and two action steps by on-policy self-distillation (OPSD). This student, however, is bounded by its teacher, which only imitates demonstrations. The second stage, Task-Specific Improvement and Merging, therefore tunes the teacher by reinforcement learning (RL) and merges the slow specialists into one few-step student by multi-teacher on-policy distillation (M-OPD). On QLM-Bench, ten simulated tasks with 1,000 demonstrations synthesized without teleoperation, the transferred policy reaches 77.0%, and the OPSD student keeps 76.7% while sampling 14 times faster. Task-specific RL and M-OPD lift it to 85.0%. We also deploy the OPSD student on a real Unitree Go2 Air. WAM-Policy thus achieves a stronger quadrupedal policy at a fraction of the inference cost.

#### 与 OPD / OPSD 的相关性依据

标题或摘要中明确出现强相关短语：on-policy self-distillation。

#### Abstract 原文

> On-policy self-distillation (OPSD) offers a promising approach to improving visual reasoning in multimodal large language models (MLLMs). However, not all samples benefit equally from privileged visual guidance, and uniform token supervision can dilute visually grounded signals. We propose VisAlloc, a dual-level framework combining sample-level visual filtering with token-level counterfactual reweighting. A fixed paired-teacher filter retains samples answered correctly from an evidence-centered positive crop but incorrectly from a spatially displaced negative crop. At the token level, both EMA teachers score the same student-generated response, and their absolute log-probability differences measure view sensitivity. Additional supervision targets tokens admitted by sensitivity or teacher-distribution disagreement, with shared frozen-prior, template-bigram, and student--teacher divergence gates. Inverse-frequency redistribution preserves the total additional weight within each micro-batch, while the positive teacher remains the sole distillation target. Inference requires only the full-image student. Across eight benchmarks, our 4B and 9B models achieve macro-averages of 81.44 and 84.92, outperforming Vision-OPD reproductions by 0.97 and 0.52 points, respectively. Code on GitHub: https://github.com/Kaelyn01/VisAlloc

### 3. Replay, Don't Resample: Off-Policy Consolidation of Long-Horizon RL Experts

- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24059
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.24059
- Authors: Zhenrui Yue, Gongxiaohui Chen, Huimin Zeng, Honglei Zhuang, Jiashuo Sun, Zhen Han, Dong Wang
- 作者单位：待全文阅读确认
- arXiv published: 2026-11-18
- arXiv updated: 2026-11-18
- Categories: cs.LG, cs.AI

#### 与 OPD / OPSD 的相关性依据

标题或摘要中明确出现强相关短语：on-policy distillation。

#### Abstract 原文

> Frontier post-training follows a common recipe: train one expert per domain with reinforcement learning (RL), then consolidate the experts into one model via multi-teacher on-policy distillation (MOPD). Such consolidation is expensive: the student samples in every environment again, an expert scores every sampled token, and the student inherits the drift of the final experts. Yet we argue that the experts' RL runs have already produced what consolidation needs: rollouts with their sampling probabilities and rewards, in the order the experts learned from them. We propose multi-expert replay policy distillation (MRPD), which replays these training logs through the student in the experts' order. At each step, the policy gradient is recomputed on these scored trajectories, and the final experts' distributions are distilled at the logged prefixes. Following the experts' own trajectory reduces the training-inference mismatch and keeps the student within the clip range, so the replay is off-policy in origin but quasi-on-policy throughout consolidation. MRPD samples nothing and calls no environment or reward model: the trainer is the sole process, and consolidating the experts costs only about a seventh of the compute used to train them. On three long-horizon agentic domains (reasoning, search and SQL), MRPD yields the best student model, substantially outperforming online RL baselines, and surpassing MOPD from the same experts at a fraction of MOPD's cost. Our analysis on the same logs shows that the replayed trajectories carry most of the gain, and that both the policy gradient and the distillation term add to it. Most interestingly, the order of the logs matters more than their amount or rewards, suggesting that expert training logs are a curriculum, not a data mixture.

### 4. HINT-WAM: Hindsight-Informed Training for World Action Models

- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24789
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.24789
- Authors: Phuong Tuan Dat, Qi Li, Xinchao Wang
- 作者单位：待全文阅读确认
- arXiv published: 2026-11-18
- arXiv updated: 2026-11-18
- Categories: cs.RO

#### 与 OPD / OPSD 的相关性依据

标题或摘要中明确出现强相关短语：on-policy self-distillation。

#### Abstract 原文

> World action models (WAMs) have shown promising generalization and data efficiency in robot manipulation by coupling action generation with predictions of future visual dynamics. Translating these capabilities into reliable task execution, however, remains a challenge for existing post-training methods. Offline supervised fine-tuning provides dense supervision on expert trajectories, but its flow-matching action expert is trained only on noised demonstration actions and never on the intermediate states produced by its own sampler; task-specific adaptation can also compromise pretrained capabilities. On-policy reinforcement learning trains on the policy's own behavior, yet sparse task rewards make exploration and credit assignment difficult, while dense reward shaping still provides scalar evaluations rather than direct action targets. We introduce \textbf{HINT-WAM}, the first on-policy self-distillation framework that supervises the action expert on its own sampling trajectories using hindsight, the future video of the demonstration, as privileged information available to the WAM itself. For each demonstration observation, the student generates its action through its own denoising trajectory, and at the states along this trajectory the same model acts as a teacher conditioned additionally on this hindsight, providing a conditional action distribution as a dense training target, while the student sees only deployment-time inputs and needs no environmental rewards or simulator interaction. To transfer this supervision to a flow-matching action expert, HINT-WAM distills the teacher into the student with an energy score, a distribution-level objective that matches the teacher's action distribution from the student's own samples rather than regressing its velocity field. The project web is available in https://tuandattt.github.io/hintwam/.

#### Abstract 原文

> On-policy distillation (OPD) is a powerful paradigm for improving language-model agents on long-horizon tasks, yet its conventional formulation relies on teacher logits that text-only black- box models do not expose. To address this limitation, we introduce Communicative On-Policy Distillation (COPD), which uses natural-language communication as a medium for distilling a stronger model's capabilities into a smaller student. During task execution, the student au- tonomously decides when to consult a black-box teacher and formulates open-ended natural- language requests without predefined question templates or intervention schedules. Successful student-teacher interactions are retained as privileged references to supervise fresh adviser-free student rollouts through on-policy self-distillation, jointly with task-reward reinforcement learning. On CAMG-II, COPD achieves 2.46 times the gain of the strongest black-box OPD baseline. The capabilities it learns through communication generalize to Terminal-Bench 2.1 and SWE-Bench Pro V2, improving task success without adviser access.
