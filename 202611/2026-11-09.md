#### Abstract 原文

> On-policy distillation (OPD) reduces train-inference distribution mismatch by supervising a student on its own trajectories. For multi-turn tool-use agents, conditioning the teacher on a validated same-task successful trajectory as a reference can provide useful hindsight, but different successes may reach incompatible interaction states and offer misleading supervision. We cast successful-reference selection as a free-target optimal transport problem, which reduces to assigning each failed trajectory its most compatible successful reference. Because latent state paths are hidden, we use action-sequence alignment as an observable proxy for compatibility and instantiate it with longest common subsequence (LCS), which identifies a shared ordered backbone of actions while allowing recoverable detours. This perspective makes trajectory compatibility the central criterion for reference selection in multi-turn OPD. Experiments on ALFWorld and the airline domain of $τ^3$-bench show that LCS is the only evaluated reference selector to improve over reference-free OPD on both ALFWorld overall scores and all three airline metrics. Without further optimization, the airline-trained models also transfer to the retail domain, where LCS improves all three metrics.

#### Abstract 原文

> Video reasoning hinges on a small number of visually decisive tokens embedded in otherwise largely language-driven chains of thought. Yet prevailing post-training methods such as GRPO overlook this structure, assigning coarse trajectory-level rewards while requiring multiple rollouts and often costly external judges. We introduce Visually-Aware On-Policy Self-Distillation (VIOS), a post-training framework that identifies where a model under-utilizes visual evidence and concentrates learning at those positions. Crucially, VIOS requires only a single on-policy rollout from a single model, without external rewards, judges, or larger teachers. Across 12 benchmarks spanning general, temporal, spatial, knowledge-intensive, and long-video understanding, VIOS consistently improves three model backbones and outperforms contemporary video-reasoning systems, while training in over 9x fewer steps, 60x fewer FLOPs, and 111x fewer generated tokens. #### Abstract 原文

> Many real-world applications require language models to internalize knowledge that was absent from pretraining, to incorporate continually updated facts, or to learn new domain skills. Self-distillation has emerged as a natural approach for this task. A model conditioned on the source material acts as the teacher, and an unconditioned student is trained to reproduce the teacher's behavior closed-book. We propose Speculative Self-Distillation (SSD), an efficient per-token mixed-policy method for self-distillation. The student generates each rollout by default, while the teacher intervenes only at positions where its next-token distribution substantially diverges from the student's. On a forgetting stress test, SSD retains the most general capability while also achieving the best in-domain accuracy and using less supervision than the on-policy baseline. Overall, SSD establishes a new accuracy-efficiency-forgetting Pareto frontier for self-distillation.

#### Abstract 原文

> Multi-teacher on-policy distillation (MOPD) merges the capabilities of multiple domain-expert teachers, such as mathematical reasoning, code generation, and instruction following, into a single compact student. Since all domains share a limited training budget, how to allocate it across domains directly determines the quality of distillation. Existing methods allocate the budget by each domain's learning progress, but offer no guarantee for lagging domains. We therefore introduce distributionally robust optimization (DRO) into MOPD to prioritize the worst-performing domain. However, the per-domain training signals are estimated from a few rollouts and are inherently noisy, so directly applying DRO mistakes noise for the worst case, lets occasional spikes hijack the budget, and protects inherently hard domains rather than truly regressing ones. Experiments show that RDS-MOPD outperforms recent competing methods and ranks first on most mathematical reasoning, code generation, and instruction-following benchmarks.

#### Abstract 原文

> Agentic reinforcement learning (RL) and multi-teacher on-policy distillation (MOPD) offer complementary routes to post-training. For visual agents, however, tool use presents two related challenges. Models may overlook useful tool-returned observations, while poorly grounded actions can limit the evidence available for subsequent distillation. #### Abstract 原文

> Reinforcement learning is now the mainstream paradigm for training LLMs on agentic tasks, yet standard practice keeps only the best-validation checkpoint and discards the rest. We show that this is lossy: in GRPO training of LLM agents, the best-validation checkpoint fails on problems that earlier checkpoints from the same run had already solved, and the union of problems solved across checkpoints far exceeds any single one. The discarded checkpoints thus form a teacher pool obtained at no additional training cost, which on-policy distillation (OPD) can mine to recover the lost coverage. Mining this pool raises two challenges: i) which checkpoint to distill from, as no checkpoint dominates the others and complementarity to the student is problem-specific; ii) where to trust it, as even a well-chosen teacher is only locally superior, so indiscriminate distillation overwrites behavior the student has already mastered. We propose Agentic-OPD, which treats the best-validation checkpoint as the student and all saved checkpoints as frozen teacher candidates, using the student's evolving capability gaps to guide both teacher selection and supervision filtering: the HCN policy (Highest Coverage Next) dynamically selects teachers by weighted coverage of the student's unsolved problems, while the LAD module (Locality-Aware Distillation) confines transfer to failed student rollouts on problems where the teacher performs better. Across five agentic task domains and 24 benchmarks and task subsets, Agentic-OPD improves over its initialization checkpoint by up to 7.4 points, with a 4B student surpassing the latest frontier models GPT-5.6 and DeepSeek-V4.1 on deep search. Agentic-OPD recasts a finished RL run as a reusable pool of policies and establishes post-RL capability recovery as a stage orthogonal to improvements in the RL algorithm itself.
