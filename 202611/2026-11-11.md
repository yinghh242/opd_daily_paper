### 1. Privileged Information Does Not Guarantee Privileged Guidance: Auditing On-Policy Self-Distillation for Reasoning Models

- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16064
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.16064
- Authors: Haoyi Qiu, Akshat Agarwal
- 作者单位：待全文阅读确认
- arXiv published: 2026-11-11
- arXiv updated: 2026-11-11
- Categories: cs.CL

#### 与 OPD / OPSD 的相关性依据

标题或摘要中明确出现强相关短语：on-policy self-distillation。

#### Abstract 原文

> On-policy self-distillation (OPSD) trains a student on next-token targets from a teacher, a frozen copy of the student that additionally receives privileged information, such as a worked solution, hidden from the student. Whether student gains reflect that information remains unclear. Taking the worked solution as privileged information, we audit four stages across three Qwen3 scales on mathematics: Source, whether the teacher can use the solution; Encoding, whether the resulting token targets are useful; Transmission, whether the student learns from the targets; and Expression, whether the student applies the learned behavior without the solution. With the solution, the teacher solves hard problems and recovers from student errors, yet the teacher does not reliably recognize errors, and its token targets do not favor correct trajectories. Repeated training makes successes within the student's reach more frequent rather than unlocking new ones, and corrections the student learns to predict are not reliably expressed in free generation. To isolate the solution's contribution, we vary its availability separately from teacher and student thinking modes. The largest gains occur when a teacher in thinking mode supervises a student trained and evaluated without an explicit thinking trace, and problem-only teachers often match or exceed privileged-solution teachers: the gain tracks the teacher's thinking mode rather than the solution, a pattern we call reasoning arbitrage. Accuracy gains alone therefore do not establish transfer of reasoning from privileged solutions: bottlenecks lie after Source, across Encoding, Transmission, and Expression. Future evaluations must measure what the solution adds to otherwise identical training.

### 2. Prior-as-Skill: Remember Outside and Read Inside with On-Policy Self-Distillation for Self-Evolving VLM Agents

- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15766
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.15766

#### 与 OPD / OPSD 的相关性依据

标题或摘要中明确出现强相关短语：on-policy self-distillation。

#### Abstract 原文

> Self-evolving visual agents keep what they learn as skills: key frames with step notes on a task's steps and their order. On-policy self-distillation (OPSD) is now a popular way to train such skills into a vision-language model that matches, on its own answers, a teacher that also reads the written order. At deployment, frames often go missing or show an unusual order, as when a person or a website reorders the steps. Existing internalization methods keep any order they store in the frame-reading weights: trained on full frames, the model guesses when they are missing, and trained with frames withheld, it stores the order, so memory can override what it sees. We propose PriorSplit, which fills in missing frames from an order kept outside the model, with no training beyond OPSD. It counts which of two neighboring steps comes first in training episodes, and the model checks if both asked steps are in view: if so, it answers from the frames, and otherwise the counts answer. Extensive experiments on synthetic skills with five models and on step-tagged order questions in new episodes of seen web, cooking and egocentric tasks demonstrate that, with frames missing, PriorSplit outperforms every compared method at OPSD's training cost. With Qwen3-VL-4B on synthetic skills, it also stays close to OPSD on unusual orders, where methods that gain most by storing the order fall behind.

### 3. Janus-MOPD: Tracing the Student Learning Dynamics in Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation

#### 与 OPD / OPSD 的相关性依据

标题或摘要中明确出现强相关短语：on-policy distillation。

#### Abstract 原文

> Multi-domain capabilities are critical for practically applicable Large Reasoning Models (LRMs). Therefore, Multi-teacher On-Policy Distillation (MOPD) has become a crucial part of the post-training process, integrating the capabilities of multiple domain-specific teacher models into a single student model. However, the student usually fails to acquire multi-domain capabilities simultaneously due to undesirable domain heterogeneity. Existing works have address this problem in view of temporal uneven efficiency and spatial hard allocation in external multi-teacher supervision. While in broader multi-domain training, varying degrees of performance degradation is still largely unexplained. In this paper, we shift the focus on how the student learns and integrates multi-domain capabilities internally, and then propose \textbf{\textsc{Janus-MOPD}}, a general learning dynamics framework of geometric representations for LRMs. Specifically, our main focus is on the internal performance of multi-domain capabilities on both temporal and spatial dimensions within the student. Temporally, our study shows that the student exhibits a Expansion-Consolidation-Optimization (ECO) three-phase learning dynamics, together with inherent limitations in learning efficiency and scaling. Spatially, we summarize two types of subspace locking within and across domains by tracing the representation space of the student, while explaining its clear learning directions but inherent cross-domain conflict. Therefore, \textsc{Janus-MOPD} yields a unified learning dynamics formulation, a geometric tracing methodology, and systematic findings of how the student learns and integrates multi-domain capabilities. Overall, we provide a \textsc{Janus} perspective to internally trace the continual evolution of the multi-domain capabilities within the model itself.
