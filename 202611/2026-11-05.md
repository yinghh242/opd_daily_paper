#### Abstract 原文

> On-policy distillation (OPD) supplies dense token-level supervision by aligning a student to the teacher's next-token distribution on its own generated rollouts. Existing works mostly implement top-$k$ OPD, applying reverse Kullback-Leibler ($\RKL$) divergence between the teacher's and the student's renormalized distributions over teacher top-$k$ support. However, the teacher scores student-generated rollouts that are inherently off-policy for itself, leaving the reliability of its next-token guidance unclear. Restricting the divergence to the teacher top-$k$ support also discards the remaining mass outside that support. These gaps leave two questions implicit: 1) which tokens to align, and 2) how to align those tokens. To answer these questions, we propose Overlap-gated On-Policy Distillation (O$^{2}$PD). As for 1), we quantitatively analyze the reliability of teacher supervision and find that alignment on the student's trajectory prefix is more reliable than alignment on the full context. Therefore, our O$^{2}$PD is applied only to the 20\% prefix tokens of each student rollout. As for 2), we theoretically characterize two problems of top-$k$ OPD: the \emph{Teacher-support Uncovering} and the \emph{Dominant-Mode Mismatch}. Based on these analyses, we propose two support strategies: the \emph{Reset Support} strategy attaches a reset atom to every token, pooling mass outside the teacher top-$k$, and the \emph{Support Convergence} strategy converges the support to the already aligned intersection. Our O$^{2}$PD employs the designed overlap-gated $α$-divergence to interpolate between mode-covering and mode-seeking for per-token alignment, where the overlap is used as a gate to switch the support and adaptively modulate $α$.

#### Abstract 原文

> On-policy distillation (OPD) is emerging as an important paradigm for language-model post-training, combining dense teacher supervision with training on student-generated trajectories. This creates a feedback loop where each update changes the student and thereby the training distribution that generates subsequent updates. Yet, how this feedback shapes learning and generalization remains poorly understood. #### Abstract 原文

> On-Policy Self-Distillation (OPSD) improves reasoning by training a student on its own reasoning, using the same model conditioned on reference solutions as a teacher. Applying OPSD to multilingual reasoning requires the teacher to provide useful feedback across languages, even when the student makes mistakes in intermediate steps. Experiments on multilingual mathematical reasoning across ten languages and three model scales show that TOPSD consistently outperforms conventional OPSD, particularly in low- and medium-resource languages.

#### Abstract 原文

> Generative recommendation retrieves items by autoregressively generating Semantic IDs(SIDs), where an RQ-VAE quantizes each item into a hierarchical SID sequence that the decoder generates level by level. We identify an overlooked bottleneck in this paradigm: the Semantic Quantization Gap(SQG). Quantization derives SIDs from rich continuous signals, including codebook centroids, local neighborhood geometry, and level-wise entropy. However, the decoder receives only discrete identifiers and loses the semantic structure that shaped them. This mismatch manifests in three ways: a grounding gap, where the decoder lacks semantic anchors when target-side information is limited; a neighborhood gap, where token-level supervision discards inter-item similarity and exacerbates exposure bias; and a difficulty gap, where computation is allocated uniformly despite generation difficulty diminishing with depth. To bridge the SQG, we propose BRIDGE. Codebook-grounded latent reasoning injects frozen quantizer centroids as semantic anchors before SID generation. Privileged EMA self-distillation transfers neighborhood-aware knowledge from a teacher to an on-policy student, preserving inter-item geometry while mitigating exposure bias. Effort-aware recurrence allocates more computation to the challenging early levels and progressively reduces it with depth. Across three Amazon benchmarks, BRIDGE improves Recall@10 and NDCG@10 by 10.9% and 9.7% on average over the strongest baseline (up to 12.6% and 10.7%). In a one-week online A/B experiment on a large-scale e-commerce advertising platform with 5% traffic, BRIDGE improves eCPM by 0.65%.
