### 1. StepX-OPD: Rethinking Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation in Diffusion Models

- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04386

- Authors: Chenru Wang, Xuanyang Zhang, Yucheng Han, Wenbo Nie, Wei Cheng, Xianfang Zeng, Gang YU, Chi Zhang

#### 与 OPD / OPSD 的相关性依据

标题或摘要中明确出现强相关短语：on-policy distillation。

#### Abstract 原文

> A deployable text-to-image diffusion model must hold several capabilities at once, such as instruction following, text rendering and human preference, and must sample in a few steps. Capabilities and few-step sampling are built in separate stages: a full-step model first consolidates capabilities through multi-teacher on-policy distillation, then serves as a fixed teacher for step distillation. Every full-step model we evaluate scores lower once compressed, and the second stage cannot begin until the first finishes. We introduce StepX-OPD, which merges the two stages: one student acquires every capability while learning to sample in a few steps, so frozen specialists supervise it on the states its own rollout visits rather than on a longer trajectory it never runs. Because a decomposition of the rollout's KL weights velocity mismatch most heavily near the clean end, we also regress the student's velocity on the low-noise part of the rollout. Separately, we find that capability integration makes models write prompt words into images on prompts that never asked for text, and we give a variant that lowers it. We evaluate on SD3.5-Medium and FLUX.1-dev across instruction following, text rendering, image quality and human preference. On both architectures, StepX-OPD attains the highest normalized average score among the four-step methods compared and exceeds the forty-step Flow-OPD model, and on SD3.5-Medium it trains with about 37% fewer GPU-hours than sequential DiffusionOPD followed by step distillation.

#### Abstract 原文

> Naive on-policy RL is often bottlenecked by the capabilities of the base model --- insufficient coverage can result in an inability to learn from certain rewards. At the same time, rewards in modern post-training systems often come from rich textual sources, e.g. judge models with a rubric of criteria. Can we utilize such structures to provide a rich directional signal that enables improving on otherwise difficult to optimize rewards? In this work, we examine a framework we refer to as targeted guidance that learns from per-turn textual feedback via an on-policy self-distillation objective. Our focus is not only on directly improving hard-to-optimize behaviors, but on how to do so throughout the training process without degrading baseline RL performance. Over a range of settings across an agentic multi-turn software engineering environment, we find success by combining targeted self-distillation with a per-criteria RL reward, and by limiting feedback to turns where the policy specifically fails. We further examine the design choices that lead to stable entropy and reduce oscillatory behavior throughout training.

### 3. Shuffled OPSD: When Semantic Consistency Hurts On-Policy Self-Distillation?

- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04958

- 作者单位：待全文阅读确认

- Categories: cs.CL, cs.AI

#### 与 OPD / OPSD 的相关性依据

标题或摘要中明确出现强相关短语：on-policy self-distillation。

#### Abstract 原文

> Knowledge distillation improves a student model's reasoning ability through supervision from a teacher. On-policy self-distillation (OPSD) uses the same model as both teacher and student under different contexts: the student generates reasoning trajectories from the question alone, while the teacher additionally receives a complete worked example and provides token-level supervision along those trajectories. Because the problem statement is shared, its content and ordering shape both student generation and teacher feedback. Preserving its semantic structure therefore seems natural, but does it always improve learning? We introduce Shuffled OPSD, which shuffles the words of the shared problem statement while keeping the teacher-only worked example intact. Across three mathematical reasoning benchmarks and three Qwen3 model scales, the method improves mean accuracy over both an intact-target control and Standard OPSD under matched training configurations. The largest gain over Standard OPSD is 3.52 percentage points on Qwen3-1.7B. Further controls show that preserving larger word blocks offers no advantage over word-level shuffling at the evaluated scales, whereas shuffling the teacher-only worked example reduces accuracy at every scale. Cross-problem mixing and target-removal experiments further distinguish the roles of problem content, word order, and teacher context. These results show that disrupting the shared problem statement can improve learning, while disrupting the teacher-only reference hurts performance. They challenge semantic coherence as a universally beneficial principle for OPSD and motivate closer study of how input structure shapes student generation and teacher supervision.

#### Abstract 原文

> The memory of production agents is increasingly becoming a bottleneck and is built almost exclusively from text: key points are extracted from out-of-limit context and retrieved into context at query time. Therefore, every new query incurs an additional cost that grows with the accumulated history. Weight memory is a complementary alternative that internalizes knowledge into model parameters and answers with no context at all, and has recently proven effective in self-evolving agents for internalizing skills and behaviors. However, its application in episodic and semantic memory, such as user-specific facts, remains comparatively unexplored, and a gap remains between earlier attempts at weight memory and text-based systems regarding recall performance. We study how to make weight memory competitive as a long-term factual store by applying on-policy self-distillation (OPSD). It trains a context-free student from a teacher, which is the same model conditioned on the user's memory, together with a verify-then-regenerate router that lets weight and text memory each cover the queries they handle best. On the HaluMem benchmark, OPSD matches or surpasses strong text-memory baselines, including full-context prompting and retrieval systems, while reading fewer tokens per query. We further conduct an extensive study that characterizes the behavior of weight memory and analyzes how different training recipes affect its performance. As agent harnesses move toward persistent, multi-session deployments, these results position weight memory as a practical, low-cost component of agent memory and provide insights into feasible training and evaluation protocols for combining it with retrieval.

#### Abstract 原文

> Larger generative retrieval models can improve retrieval quality, but their decoding cost makes low-latency serving difficult. When different users submit the same query, generating complete Semantic IDs (SIDs) for every request repeats computation, while their interaction histories call for different results. The challenge is to reuse large-model computation across requests while keeping retrieval personalized. We propose \textbf{ReuseGR}, which separates query-level candidate planning from request-level personalized completion. A 7B offline planner generates SID prefix sets shared across requests with the same query. A 0.5B online model uses each request's history to select prefixes and generate the remaining tokens. ReuseCut chooses a cache depth for each query based on historical coverage, balancing computation reuse and candidate coverage. To strengthen online completion, we transfer 7B suffix-scoring knowledge to 0.5B through path-level knowledge distillation (Path KD). BudgetCut then selects scored branches to improve final top-100 recall under a fixed branch budget. Its selection policy is initialized by on-policy distillation (OPD) and refined through group-relative policy optimization (GRPO). On an industrial search-advertising dataset with approximately 2M query--item records and 3.38M catalog items, ReuseGR improves query-level HR@100 by 3.83 percentage points and reduces mean online latency by 38.9\% relative to an all-online 0.5B baseline when prefixes are prepared. It achieves HR@100 comparable to an all-online 7B baseline with 57.7\% lower online latency. A 14-day user-level online A/B test against the 0.5B baseline shows relative gains of 0.93\% in click-through rate and 1.05\% in revenue per thousand requests.

#### Abstract 原文

> Multi-teacher on-policy distillation (MOPD) extends on-policy distillation (OPD) to consolidate capabilities from multiple teachers. Although prior work reports near-perfect in-distribution (IND) distillation performance, we find that this can mask incomplete capability consolidation beyond the supervised distribution. A preliminary study on synthetic reasoning shows that OPD does not fully transfer teachers' generalization capabilities. We then extend this investigation to real-world tasks, evaluating extrapolative and compositional generalization across mathematical reasoning, coding, agent, and long-context capabilities using Qwen3 models. Across extrapolative settings, OPD and MOPD reach 96.04-102.73% of their teachers' IND scores. Yet their out-of-distribution (OOD) scores remain 2.45-16.25% below the base model's. Across three compositional settings, MOPD improves IND performance over the base model, but its performance on composed tasks as OOD remains 18.19-24.34% below that of the best teachers. To understand these gaps, we examine teacher capability and data coverage as two potential bottlenecks. Jointly alleviating these bottlenecks eliminates OOD forgetting in our experiments and suggests the potential for generalization beyond the teacher. (M)OPD also reduces OOD forgetting on average relative to its reinforcement learning (RL) baselines. Analysis of the associated update geometry reveals smaller and more spectrally concentrated updates on average than those of the RL baselines, along with greater shared update structure under MOPD. Motivated by these findings, we propose geometry-based patching and base-preserving replay to improve OOD performance while largely preserving IND performance. Our findings identify a blind spot in capability consolidation via (M)OPD, highlighting the need for algorithms that explicitly support OOD retention and generalization.

### 7. MDSKILL: Learning to Internalize Skill Memory through Multi-View Distillation for Efficient Dynamic Agent Self-Evolution

- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05313
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05313
- Authors: Dian Jin, Jinluan Yang, Kairong Han, Baohong Li, Zijing Hu, Yi He, Wenlin Zhong, Yunze Tong, Minqin Zhu, Linchuan Li, Yanan Niu, Kun Kuang
- 作者单位：待全文阅读确认

#### 与 OPD / OPSD 的相关性依据

标题或摘要中明确出现强相关短语：on-policy self-distillation。

#### Abstract 原文

> Skill memories encode past experiences as textual prompts to improve reinforcement learning (RL) for self-evolving agents. Complementing RL, On-Policy Self-Distillation (OPSD) provides dense, fine-grained supervision to internalize knowledge from skill-guided trajectories and accelerate policy learning. However, self-generated memories often contain noisy supervision and functionally entangled, redundant, or conflicting knowledge, making direct distillation difficult. Under limited training budgets, these issues can hinder knowledge internalization and even degrade performance. To address this problem, we propose MDSKILL, a multi-view distillation approach for skill memory internalization. MDSKILL organizes skill memories into three complementary functional views (state grounding, action planning, and execution control) and selects high-quality skills using dynamic metrics to construct view-specific and integrated reasoning branches. OPSD then transfers and consolidates complementary experiential knowledge across these branches into a shared policy, enabling efficient skill internalization and continuous agent self-evolution. We evaluate MDSKILL on three agent benchmarks against RL, skill-augmented, and self-distillation baselines that do not rely on stronger external teacher models. Without additional computational overhead, MDSKILL improves upon SOTA performance by up to 7.1% and 8.1% on ALFWorld and WebShop. Extensive ablations and evolution analyses further validate its effectiveness in knowledge internalization and policy improvement.
