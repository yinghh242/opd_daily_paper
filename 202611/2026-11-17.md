#### Abstract 原文

> Detailed video captioning requires comprehensive and faithful descriptions of objects, actions, and temporal events. Fixed-frame sampling, however, can miss brief motions, small objects, and other fine-grained evidence, motivating captioning agents that actively seek relevant moments and inspect local regions. Training such behavior is challenging because, unlike video question answering, generic captioning instructions do not specify what visual evidence should be acquired. We propose Perception-Question On-Policy Distillation (PQ-OPD), which uses training-only Perception Questions (PQs) to guide active video perception. PQs specify what should be inspected without revealing the corresponding answers, encouraging the teacher to explore the video more comprehensively and focus on relevant visual evidence. Along trajectories generated by the student, on-policy distillation transfers this guidance to the student's reasoning, tool calls, and captions; no PQs are required at inference. Across three detailed-captioning and two caption-to-QA benchmarks, PQ-OPD consistently improves the corresponding untrained agents across teacher-student scales and input-frame budgets. Under our evaluation protocol, PQ-OPD achieves the strongest primary-metric results among the compared open-source models on DREAM-1K, CaReBench, ShortVidBench, MotionBench, and VidCapBench-AE. Our ablations, together with analysis of temporal coverage and answer leakage, further indicates that the student internalizes the training-time guidance as a more effective video exploration policy.

#### Abstract 原文

> In on-policy distillation (OPD), response completion, supervision delivery, and parameter updates need not share the same granularity. We develop streaming OPD by decoupling these three clocks: a response repeatedly delivers newly generated segments while generation continues, retaining the full causal prefix at each delivery and computing loss only on new positions. A consumption protocol then organizes supervision budgets, position selection, staleness handling, and update scheduling, allowing control over quantity, coverage, and version lag across training stages. In GSM8K experiments distilling Qwen3-4B into Qwen3-0.6B with VERL, supervised-budget matching reduces the early gap between best test accuracies over seven common candidates from 4.55 to 2.42 points. Synchronous continuation from the same checkpoint reaches 73.39% best test accuracy over seven common candidates at u=384,...,576, versus 74.30% synchronous and 71.95% budget-matched (differences -0.91 and -2.35 points); an unpaired extended record reaches 76.12%. Freshness filtering and disagreement-overlap selection improve local peaks but do not establish sustained advantages. A system prototype shows lower data-supply waiting times without establishing an equal-update speedup. Quality recovery and supply-side benefits are assessed separately; end-to-end gains must account for continuation cost.

#### Abstract 原文

> Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation (MOPD) enables a single student to absorb the skills of several domain teachers by learning from its own generated responses. We present Multimodal Model Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation Reasoner (M3OPD-Reasoner), a fully open recipe for post-training vision-language models (VLMs) over three reasoning domains: image STEM, image perception, and video. We curate two datasets, M3OPD-SFT-1.35M for supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and M3OPD-RL-193K for reinforcement learning (RL) with verifiable rewards. On these data, we compare post-training recipes that start from Qwen3.5-9B: training one model on the mixed data of all domains (MixSFT and MixRL), and training one teacher per domain with Group Sequence Policy Optimization (GSPO), directly or after an SFT warm-up, and distilling the teachers into one student with MOPD. We evaluate on 21 image STEM, image perception, and video reasoning benchmarks after we decontaminate the training data against them. The comparison establishes MOPD of teachers trained with GSPO directly from Qwen3.5-9B as the best recipe: the student closely matches each teacher on its own domain and is on average above the mixed-training baselines on the same prompts. Since MOPD already merges the domain capabilities that MixRL would otherwise have to learn, its student is a stronger starting point for RL, and we ask whether the same data can carry the model further from there: we add MixRL on top of the MOPD student, on the same prompts and with the same settings, which, to our knowledge, no prior VLM recipe has studied. The same prompts still improve the MOPD student, with no collapse, and give our main model, M3OPD-Reasoner-9B. MOPD and MixRL can therefore work as consecutive stages that get more out of the same data. We release all data, the training code, and the checkpoints of every stage.

#### 与 OPD / OPSD 的相关性依据

标题或摘要中明确出现强相关短语：on-policy distillation。

#### Abstract 原文

> Conventional knowledge distillation can facilitate the propagation of backdoors from teacher models to student models. However, how this risk manifests in On-Policy Distillation (OPD) remains insufficiently studied. OPD has recently emerged as an important post-training paradigm for Vision-Language Models (VLMs). Unlike traditional offline distillation, OPD does not require teacher-generated responses. Instead, it uses the teacher's probability distributions to supervise response trajectories generated online by the student. Since the initial student model typically generates benign responses and receives no explicit supervision from malicious target responses, OPD appears to offer some inherent resistance to backdoor propagation. In this paper, we challenge this intuition and reveal a more subtle risk of visual backdoor propagation in OPD. Through experiments across multiple model families and datasets, together with theoretical analysis, we identify three main findings. First, unlike the relatively gradual propagation observed in traditional offline distillation, backdoors rarely emerge in OPD at low poisoning ratios. However, the attack success rate rapidly approaches 100% once a critical threshold is exceeded. Second, backdoor propagation does not depend on explicit malicious responses. Instead, it is driven by the continuous accumulation of anomalous probability mass assigned by the teacher. Finally, based on these findings, we propose a novel trigger design that enables visual backdoor propagation with zero explicit trigger injection. These findings further reveal an overlooked mechanism of backdoor propagation in OPD and highlight its potential security risks.
