### 1. TeachBack: Learning to Teach via Bidirectional Preference Optimization for On-Policy Self-Distillation

- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29167

- Authors: Linfei Ji, Jiayun Li, Haozhao Wang, Siyu Jiang, Zehao Wang, Longtao Xiao, Yu-Jie Yuan, Ruixuan Li

#### 与 OPD / OPSD 的相关性依据

标题或摘要中明确出现强相关短语：on-policy self-distillation。

#### Abstract 原文

> On-policy self-distillation (OPSD) enables a language model to learn from a Teacher instantiated from the same model but given access to privileged information, such as a reference solution. We find that access to sufficient privileged information allows the Teacher to generate highly accurate trajectories; however, accuracy alone does not guarantee that these trajectories are suitable for Student learning. Direct access to a complete solution may encourage shortened, reference-conditioned derivations that omit the exploration, verification, and self-correction required to solve problems without privileged information. We introduce TeachBack, a bidirectional preference-learning framework for on-policy self-distillation that learns not only from the Teacher but also how the Teacher should teach. TeachBack shapes the Teacher's generation behavior by controlling the length of the reference-reasoning suffix injected into the prompt and by varying the prompt style. While maintaining comparable Teacher accuracy, it encourages the Teacher to explore and re-solve problems, thereby reducing its reliance on the reference solution. At each rollout refresh, the shared policy generates both a privileged Teacher trajectory and a problem-only Student trajectory. A pairwise preference objective uses privileged Teacher trajectories to improve Student learning, while the reversed preference uses the Student's behavior to shape future Teacher supervision under the privileged condition. Experiments on mathematical and logical reasoning tasks show that TeachBack consistently improves Student performance over existing OPSD methods and related baselines.

### 2. Right On Q: Recovering Reasoning under Quantization via Self-Generated Trajectories

标题或摘要中明确出现强相关短语：on-policy distillation。

#### Abstract 原文

> Quantization-aware training (QAT) aims to recover reasoning capabilities in low-bit large language models (LLMs). Existing recipes typically supervise the quantized student with the original full-precision model on fixed response sequences. However, the resulting low-bit quantized models still struggle on long-horizon reasoning tasks and often fail to terminate within a token budget that suffices for the full-precision model. We identify two challenges in closing this gap. First, fixed-response supervision leaves a mismatch between training prefixes and those encountered during student generation, and this trajectory divergence grows as responses lengthen. Second, on its own prefixes, the student underpredicts some next-token candidates and overpredicts others, and this distributional misalignment becomes more frequent at high-entropy prefixes. We present OnQ, a low-bit recovery pipeline that combines on-policy distillation and candidate-wise Dynamic KL. Starting from block-wise calibration, the quantized student generates responses and receives full-precision teacher supervision on its own prefixes. Dynamic KL weights forward and reverse KL by each candidate's teacher-student probability ratio to balance correction of under- and overprediction. Across Qwen3-1.7B, 4B, and 8B at 2- and 3-bit precision, OnQ achieves the highest nine-benchmark average, with the largest gains on long-horizon reasoning benchmarks. At 2 bits, it outperforms every baseline on every benchmark at every model size, improving the Qwen3-8B average by 10.26 points over the strongest baseline. It also produces shorter responses with fewer truncations and repetitive errors.

#### Abstract 原文

> Visual mathematical reasoning can fail before reasoning begins: when a vision-language model (VLM) misreads a diagram, subsequent reasoning proceeds from false premises. This perceptual bottleneck is especially pronounced in smaller models, while accurate textual descriptions can substantially narrow the gap to larger models. Because obtaining such descriptions at inference requires additional annotation or model calls, we propose minimal-edit corrective on-policy distillation. Using reference figure descriptions and verified reference solutions as training-only information, a teacher scores incorrect student rollouts under a minimal-edit objective, and only teacher-disfavored tokens contribute to the corrective loss. Correct rollouts receive task-reward reinforcement learning. This preserves on-policy supervision while avoiding full-response imitation. Across model scales, the method requires no descriptions at test time and meets or exceeds the corresponding base model supplied with a reference figure description at inference. It also improves perception-oriented performance, particularly low-level geometric perception, and transfers to external mathematics and perception benchmarks. Because the minimal-edit teacher evaluates corrections rather than generating its own solution, the method remains effective with a frozen same-size teacher, although its gains are smaller than with a larger teacher on most metrics.

### 4. Asymmetric On-Policy Distillation Learns In-Context Exploration

- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.30105

#### 与 OPD / OPSD 的相关性依据

标题或摘要中明确出现强相关短语：on-policy distillation。

#### Abstract 原文

> Sequential decision-making under partial observability is difficult because agents must explore to infer hidden, task-relevant information before acting effectively. Learning such information-gathering behaviors tabula rasa via online reinforcement learning is sample-inefficient, whereas offline imitation averages expert actions across hidden states and fails to learn exploration. In this work, we propose ASTEROID, a simple and effective student--teacher supervised learning framework that can learn test-time exploration. Although a privileged expert never needs to explore, we demonstrate that a history-conditioned student can learn to do so when trained with on-policy contexts: the student first collects on-policy context under partial observability, and the privileged expert provides action labels conditioned on that context. By distilling expert actions directly conditioned on the student's on-policy history, the student learns to systematically resolve partial observability, i.e., search while uncertain and exploit once the hidden state is revealed. This enables efficient test-time exploration (similar to Bayesian posterior sampling) without incurring the impractical training-data costs of reinforcement learning. Across a diverse range of eleven different environments, including discrete exploration benchmarks, robotic manipulation, navigation, and Procgen environments, ASTEROID achieves ~2x higher returns under fixed exploration budgets, uses ~100x fewer interactions than RL baselines, and outperforms frontier models at exploration. Finally, our method transfers policies trained in simulation to vision-denied real-world robot manipulation, exhibiting efficient exploration and increasing success by +40% over standard distillation. Overall, it provides a simple yet surprisingly effective framework to learn efficient test-time exploration.
