#### Abstract 原文

> On-policy self-distillation provides dense supervision for reasoning models via a privileged self-teacher. However, this supervision reflects local token preferences rather than the expected return of an unguided student continuation. Recent methods combine this signal with GRPO. They use GRPO for update directions and the teacher for signal scaling. However, we demonstrate a more effective integration within PPO. Specifically, we identify complementary positional dynamics: privileged information enhances a critic's value-prediction accuracy toward the response's end, whereas self-teacher guidance is strong initially, drops mid-generation, and rebounds finally. These insights motivate Value-Augmented Self-Distillation Policy Optimization (VASPO), wherein a self-teacher and critic evaluate identical privileged information at student-generated prefixes. The critic computes token-level PPO advantages from predicted continuation returns, while the self-teacher modulates selected positive advantages via bounded probability ratios. Across six mathematical reasoning benchmarks, VASPO improves mean accuracy over PPO by 2.59 and 1.81 percentage points on Qwen3-4B and OLMo3-7B variants, respectively, and achieves the highest mean accuracy among evaluated methods on Knights and Knaves. Our code is available at https://github.com/cii030/VASPO.

### 2. SERO: Learning from Executable Oracle Continuations for Long-Horizon Agentic Reinforcement Learning

- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19397

- 作者单位：待全文阅读确认

- Categories: cs.AI

#### 与 OPD / OPSD 的相关性依据

标题或摘要中明确出现强相关短语：on-policy distillation。

#### Abstract 原文

> Long-horizon agentic RL is constrained by the futures visited by the current policy. When rollouts fail to reach useful behaviors, improved credit assignment cannot provide supervision for states and actions that were never encountered. On-policy distillation (OPD) introduces teacher guidance on policy-induced states, and recent multi-turn variants improve how this guidance is scheduled, replayed, refined, or validated. However, they do not directly use the oracle-controlled future itself as the training trajectory. We introduce \textbf{State-Rooted Executable Rollout Optimization (SERO)}, which augments on-policy agentic RL with executable oracle continuations. SERO restores a state actually reached by the current policy and lets a training-only oracle continue interacting with the real environment, making the downstream observations and oracle actions themselves available for learning. It then applies \textbf{policy-reachability weighting}, which weights each oracle step by the frozen behavior policy's compatibility with the preceding oracle actions. This allows deeper supervision to become effective as the policy becomes more compatible with the oracle branch. The oracle loss is optimized separately from the base RL estimator and no oracle is used at inference time. We evaluate SERO on two challenging interactive-agent benchmarks, ALFWorld and WebShop, using Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct. On ALFWorld, learning the full executable continuation is substantially more effective than supervising only the root oracle action, while policy-reachability weighting further improves out-of-distribution performance.

#### Abstract 原文

> Reinforcement Learning on Mixed-domain preference data (Mix-RL) has become the mainstream approach for training general-purpose Generative Reward Models (GRMs). However, Mix-RL suffers from two critical bottlenecks: the reward sparsity induced by outcome rewards, and the cross-domain interference arising from jointly optimizing heterogeneous capabilities, which together give rise to imbalanced capability acquisition and pervasive reward hacking. #### Abstract 原文

> On-policy distillation (OPD) trains a student model on its own rollouts with token-level supervision from a teacher, assuming that the teacher can reach a correct answer from any prefix the student writes. We call this property the recoverability of the teacher and show it fails on incorrect rollouts: the teacher follows student errors, although it recognizes them. On two Qwen3 students and six mathematical reasoning benchmarks, CriticOPD improves OPD, with the largest gains on competition problems.

#### Abstract 原文

> On-policy distillation (OPD) transfers knowledge from a teacher model to a student along student-generated trajectories, typically by minimizing the reverse Kullback-Leibler (RKL) divergence. However, RKL can provide weak corrective signals for teacher-supported tokens to which the student assigns very low probability. This raises two questions: which positions are most consequential for the behavioral gap between the student and teacher, and what additional supervision is needed at these positions? Through cross-sampling, we find that divergence-based routing more effectively pinpoints positions contributing to the teacher-student performance gap than entropy-based routing. Experiments across six math benchmarks show that DrOPD improves over standard OPD and entropy-based routing. DrOPD also reduces severe under-coverage cases by 37.9 percent relative to OPD, supporting targeted FKL as an effective complement to OPD.

#### Abstract 原文

> Reasoning language models increasingly rely on test-time compute scaling, yet most existing work studies how to allocate inference computation while treating the model itself as fixed. ### 9. ActionOPD: Distilling Decisions, Not Tokens

- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19820

#### 与 OPD / OPSD 的相关性依据

标题或摘要中明确出现强相关短语：on-policy distillation。

#### Abstract 原文

> Distilling capable language-model agents into smaller models requires aligning the student's behavior with the teacher's. This raises a fundamental question: at which states and at what granularity should this alignment be performed? Offline imitation provides dense but token-specific supervision and can suffer from state-distribution mismatch. Token-level on-policy distillation (OPD) addresses this mismatch but typically requires teacher token probabilities and compatible tokenization. We argue that semantic actions provide a natural unit of supervision for agent distillation. We introduce ActionOPD, an action-level on-policy distillation method motivated by maximizing expected teacher-action likelihood at student-encountered states. At each such state, ActionOPD samples actions from both the student and a black-box teacher, rewarding student actions according to their semantic agreement with the sampled teacher actions. Through policy optimization, ActionOPD aligns the student's action-level behavior with the teacher's decisions without requiring reproduction of teacher-written reasoning or access to teacher token probabilities. Training Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B with two teachers on diverse, curated tool-use tasks yields average gains of 3.3-6.9 percentage points across four out-of-distribution benchmarks. ActionOPD scales favorably with student size and teacher capability, without requiring the teacher and student to share a model family or tokenizer. On $τ^2$-bench, ActionOPD achieves competitive performance against offline distillation and token-level OPD. We further explore Self-ActionOPD with a same-model teacher and privileged context. These results establish ActionOPD as a promising approach to agent distillation.
