### 1. When to Follow the Teacher: Advantage-Based On-Policy Distillation

- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31563
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.31563
- Authors: Mahdi Sabbaghi, George Pappas, Hamed Hassani, Adel Javanmard
- 作者单位：待全文阅读确认

#### 与 OPD / OPSD 的相关性依据

标题或摘要中明确出现强相关短语：on-policy distillation。

#### Abstract 原文

> Online Policy Distillation (OPD) often outperforms offline methods such as Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) by training on trajectories sampled directly from the student. However, standard OPD trains the student to follow the teacher at every state of the trajectory, assuming that teacher guidance is always beneficial. We instead distinguish between what the teacher prefers and what actually helps the current student succeed. We introduce Advantage-Based On-Policy Distillation (AOPD), which weights teacher actions by their counterfactual advantage under the student. Teacher actions that improve the student's expected outcome are reinforced, while those that do not are downweighted. Directly computing these advantages requires trying alternative teacher actions at each step and running new rollouts from each. Instead, we show that the AOPD objective can be estimated using only standard student rollouts and their terminal rewards. Our estimator uses teacher importance weights on the student-sampled actions to provide unbiased advantage-weighted supervision, without any additional rollouts or reward evaluations. This allows AOPD to focus teacher supervision on actions that help the current student while preserving the rollout efficiency of standard OPD. Experiments on math, coding, and Medqa show gains over distillation and RL baselines in both external-teacher and self-distillation settings.

#### Abstract 原文

> Agentic recommendation pursues shopper goals through sequences of context-dependent decisions. Developing and evaluating such policies requires faithful recommendation world models that capture both the interactive shopping environment and users' behavioral responses over long interactions. Existing resources rarely provide long-horizon, fine-grained real-user trajectories or support trajectory-level evaluation and offline validation against production A/B results. We introduce UserArena, a real-world e-commerce interactive benchmark containing 64,729 session trajectories from 11,010 users, spanning 566,102 items and 13 action types. Experiments show that OPTD achieves the strongest overall fidelity among the evaluated simulators under UserArena's evaluation protocol and most closely reproduces the production policy effect in offline simulation.

#### Abstract 原文

> Large language models trained on data generated by other models can sometimes inherit traits unrelated to that data, a phenomenon called subliminal learning. We study both hard label distillation, where students train on numbers generated by a biased teacher, and on-policy soft label distillation, where students generate the data and the teacher's logits provide the learning signal. Using Pythia, PolyPythia, and DataDecide OLMo models, we find that transfer persists in both settings across differences in weight initialization, pretraining data order, pretraining corpus, model size, and architecture. Across pretraining checkpoints, the ability to transmit a preference and the ability to acquire one develop separately: some checkpoints are more effective teachers but weaker students, and vice versa. Consistent with this distinction, cross-model transfer can be strongly asymmetric in magnitude when teacher and student roles are reversed. To investigate what carries the preference signal, we construct synthetic number sequences that match the teacher's generated number frequencies but have no dependence on the prompt or preceding numbers and find that these still induce substantial transfer in the hard-label setting. In top-$K$ soft-label distillation, removing animal tokens from the loss reduces transfer, although not completely in the same-model setting. In our experiments, soft label distillation produces more consistent transfer with less extensive training than hard label distillation. The growing use of on-policy distillation in model post-training makes this a particularly relevant setting for assessing unintended preference transfer.

- 作者单位：待全文阅读确认

#### 与 OPD / OPSD 的相关性依据

标题或摘要中明确出现强相关短语：on-policy distillation。

#### Abstract 原文

> On-policy distillation has been widely adopted for alleviating the distribution mismatch of off-policy distillation. Self-distillation further removes the need for an external teacher by using the student's own outputs, enhanced with extra context or demonstrations, as supervision. This teacher-free design makes on-policy self-distillation practical, yet its privacy risks remain largely unexplored. In particular, using two coupled views of the same training example may strengthen its memorization. Motivated by this, we present the first study of membership inference attacks against on-policy self-distillation. We investigate both gray-box and black-box threat models and propose a unified attack framework, named OP-Print. OP-Print builds on our finding that the supervision imprint in on-policy self-distillation is sparse and structured, concentrating on uncertainty-sensitive correction sites rather than across the entire rollout. Exploiting this property, we construct attack signals tailored to the two access levels: in the gray-box setting, OP-Print directly aggregates the privileged teacher-student residual correction along the same rollout; in the black-box setting, it measures restart-based output-basin stability as an observable surrogate for the hidden correction process. Across diverse models and distillation schemes, all baseline attacks remain essentially at random guessing even under gray-box access, whereas OP-Print is broadly validated to be effective in both gray-box and black-box settings.

### 6. One Teacher, Many Views: Reducing Reference Sensitivity in On-Policy Self-Distillation

- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30880
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.30880
- Authors: Khouloud Saadi, Di Wang
- 作者单位：待全文阅读确认
- arXiv published: 2026-10-22
- arXiv updated: 2026-10-22
- Categories: cs.LG, cs.CL

#### 与 OPD / OPSD 的相关性依据

标题或摘要中明确出现强相关短语：on-policy self-distillation。

#### Abstract 原文

> On-policy self-distillation (OPSD) improves reasoning by training a model on its own trajectories with dense token-level supervision from a privileged copy conditioned on a reference solution. Existing methods typically use a single teacher distribution conditioned on the complete reference. We identify a limitation of this formulation: a reference is only one realization of the reasoning process. Although it provides evidence about the answer, it also encodes an ordering, phrasing, and solution path. Full-reference conditioning can therefore produce correct targets that depend excessively on information unavailable to the student at inference. We show that replacing one valid reference with another, while holding the problem and student state fixed, can substantially alter the teacher distribution, a phenomenon we call reference-induced target instability. To address this instability, we introduce Monte Carlo Privileged Distillation (MCPD), which models privileged context as a distribution over partial reference views and distills the marginal teacher distribution. MCPD evaluates the teacher under multiple views and averages the resulting next-token distributions. We show that this probability-space average is the exact target induced by minimizing the expected teacher-to-student KL divergence over privileged views. Across mathematical and scientific reasoning benchmarks, we show that MCPD produces more reference-consistent supervision and consistently improves over full-reference OPSD.

- 作者单位：待全文阅读确认

#### 与 OPD / OPSD 的相关性依据

标题或摘要中明确出现强相关短语：on-policy distillation。

#### Abstract 原文

> When a user says "Keep it short", how short should the LLM's response be? More generally, users and applications require different levels of instruction adherence, but prompting does not reliably provide fine-grained control over them. We therefore study "steerable language models" that provide fine-grained numerical control over the degree to which models follow general instructions specified in text. However, existing approaches do not jointly offer fine-grained control, efficiency, and scalability across diverse instructions. To address this gap, we introduce DIAL (Distilled Instruction-Adherence Levels) to achieve all three properties. For fine-grained control, we perform classifier-free guidance (CFG) with a frozen LLM to provide training signals for on-policy distillation of instruction adherence at different levels. For efficiency, we design a lightweight steering adapter that uses a classifier over the LLM's hidden states to compute a closed-form steering edit. The classifier adaptively sets the steering magnitude at each token based on the target adherence level, while accounting for the geometry of the LLM's hidden states to preserve response coherence. For scalability, the classifier is text-conditioned, allowing a shared adapter to support new instructions without per-instruction fitting, and training requires only instruction and request text. Evaluation across multiple LLM families shows that DIAL achieves a competitive balance between concept expression and response fluency, with concept scores increasing monotonically across adherence levels. We introduce SteerIF, an instruction-only dataset of 98, 625 concept, persona, verifiable, and descriptive instructions. When trained on SteerIF, DIAL provides fine-grained control over personas and, when combined with prompting, improves verifiable instruction following.
